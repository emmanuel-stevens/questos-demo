The Beta Covenant

QuestOS — last updated 20 August 2026

This page exists because the interface deliberately will not tell you. QuestOS never prints a model name on a screen where the squire is speaking — an engine that gets announced has begun to be a character, and the squire is an office, not a model. So the truth lives here instead, in plain writing, where you can read it whenever you like.

Which engine answers you today

WhereEngineWhy
The squire's bar, the Chronicler, the Sounding Board, the Rehearsal Claude Sonnet 5 The everyday voice. Fast, and enough for counsel and filing.
The Round Table — the council and the Trial Claude Opus 5 The ceremony. Five seats arguing from your real numbers is where a weaker engine shows instantly, so the heaviest room gets the strongest engine.
The founder's own realm Claude Opus 5 throughout He pays for it.

These are Anthropic models, reached through Anthropic's API. Nothing else runs an engine on your behalf. The MCP door — where your own Claude Code walks into your realm — calls no model at all: it reads and it writes, and your client does the thinking.

What is sent, and what is not

  • A portion, not a realm. Each turn sends a written summary of your day — the steps on today's board, their titles and their state — assembled by your own browser and capped in size. The whole realm is never sent.
  • No transcript is kept. The conversation lives in the page you are looking at and dies on reload. The realm is stateless by design: every turn re-briefs the engine from your ledger, which is also why swapping engines costs you nothing.
  • The verbs come back unexecuted. When the squire says a step is done, the model returns words; your browser resolves them against your real ledger and writes. A model cannot write to your realm directly. This is why an engine having a bad day produces a confused sentence rather than a corrupted record.
  • Nothing is used to train anything. Not by QuestOS.

Bringing your own engine

The plan is that you supply the mind and QuestOS supplies the realm. The coin buys the realm — the art, the order, the visible progress, the filing — and never metered AI. When engine-binding ships you will be able to point QuestOS at Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, or an open-weight model on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and the Engine Plate in your Quarters will say which one is bound and what it can do.

It will state facts and it will never grade. No engine will be called weak, worse or better. It will say things like “this engine has no effort knob, so the realm does not set one” or “this engine takes one verb at a time” — and you will draw your own conclusion.

Why the interface stays quiet about it

Two rules pull against each other and this page is how they are reconciled. A knight should be able to choose an engine; the squire should never announce one. So the choosing and the truth-telling happen here and on the Engine Plate, and the rooms where the squire speaks stay in one voice — the office's voice, whoever is powering it that day.
